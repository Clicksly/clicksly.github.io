<h1>About</h1>

<p>
  I studied computer information technology first and psychology second, and finished with a B.S. in psychological science from Northern Kentucky University. Both halves ended up mattering. Research methods taught me to design a question I could actually answer, and the CIT coursework meant I could build the thing that answered it.
</p>
<p>
  At Epic I owned test strategy for the backend and UI layers of a national health data integration, wrote the internal and customer-facing documentation for it, and reviewed designs for WCAG conformance. That last part meant checking how the software behaved for someone actually using it, which isn't the same as running a checker over a finished page.
</p>
<p>
  Years earlier I worked the special services desk at a Home Depot. One of our regulars was deaf, and after a while I got tired of both of us guessing, so I taught myself enough sign language to do the job properly. It's the same instinct, and I've been following it a while.
</p>
<p>
  I'm certified in EpicCare Ambulatory. Outside work I take photographs, including astrophotography, run a home automation setup that's more complicated than it needs to be, and do baseball analytics nobody asked for.
</p>
